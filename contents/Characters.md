# Characters

Every character in Làng Vọng stands for one kind of silence: something unsaid, unseen, or unheard. Silhouettes should be readable at a glance, and clothing grounded in a 1990s north-central Vietnamese village: *áo bà ba*, conical hats, bare feet and plastic sandals. Nothing gory.

| Character | Role | The silence they stand for | Where players meet them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sen | The ma da; the heart of the story | Never seen, never named | Reflections, camera footage, the carp steps, the finale |
| Bà Thu | Sen's mother; storyteller; key to the true ending | What she refuses to say: her daughter is dead | The shrine, from Night 1 |
| Sen's father (the Quiet Man) | The source of the silence | The man who chose to say nothing | Silenced houses, only as sound and shadow |
| Bin | The friend who gets taken | The one the players are trying to reach | Night 1; then in footage and the finale |
| The players | The ghost-hunting channel | The first ones who listen | Everywhere |
| Minh, Hạnh, Tí, Khoa | The four lost children | Children nobody watched | Memories, mementos, the drowned festival |
| The teacher, the puppet master, the fish seller, the village head | Adults of the old village | People who noticed and stayed quiet | Only through their houses and belongings |

## Sen

- **Look:** small and thin, in a faded, soaked *áo bà ba*. Long hair tangled with river weeds, hiding most of her face. One plastic sandal; the other is the one her father took from the steps.
- **Personality:** a lonely eight-year-old, not a monster. Curious, shy, desperate for company. What makes her frightening is that she doesn't understand she's hurting anyone.
- **What she wants:** to be named, remembered, and held. She does not want to be born into another family.
- **How she moves:** stillness, then sudden closeness. She appears where the players aren't looking, and is clearest in reflections and on camera.
- **Sound:** humming a children's tune, dripping water, the muffled giggles of children under the lake.

## Bà Thu

- **Look:** small and bent, gray hair in a bun, a worn *áo bà ba*. Her hands are always busy folding paper lanterns, and lanterns surround her.
- **Personality:** warm, hospitable, a little forgetful. She repeats her stories with small changes. Whenever Sen comes up, she smiles and changes the subject.
- **What she wants:** for Sen to come home. Underneath, forgiveness for her own silence.
- **The secret:** she has been dead for years. She never appears on camera, her tea is cold, and her cups are thick with dust.
- **Voice:** soft and slow, with a regional accent from north-central Vietnam if voice acting is used.

## Sen's father: the Quiet Man

- **Look:** never clearly shown. A tall shape in a doorway with its back turned, a work jacket, a hand on a radio dial.
- **What he is:** the silence itself. Sen won't let him near her, so he's stuck in the houses, inside the quiet he chose.
- **Sound:** a radio crackling louder, heavy footsteps that stop when you stop.
- **Rule:** he is never seen face to face, and the story never explains him beyond what the tale says.

## Bin

- **Look:** the most colorful of the group: bright jacket, headlamp, the camcorder always in his hand.
- **Personality:** cheerful and fearless, the one who always wanders off for a better shot.
- **Role:** his disappearance starts the real story, and his camera becomes the players' most important tool.

## The teller

- **Who she is:** an old woman who lived three doors down from Sen's family as a young wife. She turned up her radio like everyone else, promised Thu she'd look in on Sen and didn't, and was told to sit down when she finally asked about Sen at the vote.
- **Role:** narrates the cut scenes, telling the story to her grandson on Rằm night while they fold lanterns. She knows the ending from Bin's video.
- **Voice:** warm, plain, a little dry, full of small asides. She says when she saw something and when she's guessing. Her guilt sits under every line.
- **Detail:** every year she makes one more lantern than she has dead. In the last scene, her grandson writes Sen's name on it.

## The players

The four members of the channel. The simplest option is to let players keep their own Roblox avatars, with a shared channel name and outfit. A later option is to give them light roles (host, researcher, sound, camera) with small differences, such as who can read old handwriting faster.

## The lost children

- **Minh, 11:** the teacher's son. Serious, glasses, afraid of disappointing his father.
- **Hạnh, 9:** the puppet master's daughter. Imaginative, always playing pretend with puppets under the stage.
- **Tí, 6:** the fish seller's son. Small and restless, always blowing his clay whistle.
- **Khoa, 12:** the village head's son. Quiet, patient, likes to fish alone.

In the drowned festival they sit with Sen, watching a puppet show that never ends. They don't attack; they watch, and sometimes help, by making lanterns flicker toward clues.
