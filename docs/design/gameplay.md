# Làng Vọng — Game Design

Nov 1, 2026 · @catlyn

How Làng Vọng plays. The story itself lives in Doc. Threats, the timeline and the release plan each have their own tab, as notes to discuss.

## Understanding, not locks

After Night 1, every place is open, including Sen's house. Players who wander in early find a sad, ordinary home; none of it means anything yet. Once they know the story, they come back and see the same objects differently. The ending is never explained. It only becomes obvious once players have pieced together five things:

| What they learn | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| The girl is **Sen**, from the family nobody talked about | Teacher's house: a class photo with her name. Village head's house: the list of lost children made for the shrine, and Sen isn't on it. |
| Who is **waiting** for her | Bà Thu's lanterns each carry a lost child's name, and none says Sen. Bà Thu's chair and teacups are in Sen's house. And she never appears on camera. |
| **Where** Sen is | The lantern puzzle |
| That Bà Thu was **lied to**, and what will convince her | Fish seller's house: the letter she handed Sen's father, and "Don't mention this to Thu." The letter itself is hidden under the altar in Sen's house. |
| What Sen **wants** | Bà Thu's bedtime story (heard on Night 1, understood later), Sen's flashbacks with the carp she named, the missing height marks and missing name, her mother's unfinished fish lantern |

**Each place points to another.** A child's notebook mentions a friend from another house; a report is about a girl in another family; a diary describes something strange on the lake. Players move because the clues pull them, never because a counter says "2 of 4."

**If players get stuck:** Bà Thu repeats her stories with small new details, and taken children make lanterns flicker near places with clues still to find.

## The lantern puzzle

The festival's own rule is that a lantern guides a soul to its home. Every night, Bà Thu's lanterns drift to the shore below each lost child's house and wake it: the window glows, and the child's memories start to play inside. Until then, a house is dark and silent, but never locked.

Players film this on Night 1 without knowing what it means. Once they learn the rule and notice the one house that is always dark, they've found the home of the one soul nobody guides: Sen's house. Its back yard is the only way down to the carp steps from Bin's footage. The puppet master's diary backs it up: on the night Sen fell, he saw one lantern drift out from behind that house.

## The four houses

Each house holds one lost child's story, one piece of Sen's story, and a lead to somewhere else.

| House | Child | Piece of Sen's story | Leads to | Memento |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teacher's house | Minh, 11 | A class photo naming Sen, and the teacher's unsent report that something was wrong at home, noting her father keeps the family away from festivals | Sen's house; Minh's notebook mentions his friend Hạnh | A report card with one red mark |
| Puppet master's house | Hạnh, 9 | The diary: on the night Sen fell, a lantern drifted out from behind the last house on the lane | Sen's house, and what lies behind it | A broken water puppet |
| Fish seller's house | Tí, 6 | Her notes: the letter from the city she handed Sen's father, and "Don't mention this to Thu" | Sen's house, to find the letter | A clay whistle |
| Village head's house | Khoa, 12 | The minutes of the vote to leave (someone asked about Sen's family and was told to keep quiet), and the shrine list without Sen's name | The shrine; the teacher | A festival drumstick |

Picking up a memento plays a short scene of that child's last day.

## The camera and Bà Thu

**The camera** is the group's main tool. Recorded footage, played back, shows what their eyes don't: Sen's wet footprints, the drowned children standing in the water, and the midpoint reveal that Bà Thu isn't in any of the footage. It has limited battery, recharged at the shrine.

**Bà Thu in play:**

- **Storyteller:** she talks about the lost children and the old festival, and on Night 1 tells her bedtime story without explaining why it matters.
- **Safe zone:** the shrine is the one place Sen won't come.
- **A red flag:** whenever her own daughter comes up, she smiles and says Sen is with relatives.
- **The key to the true ending:** once players give her the letter, she accepts the truth, and the players lead her through her own back yard to the carp steps.

## Co-op and death

- **Taken, not killed:** a player Sen catches wakes in the drowned festival under the middle of the lake. From there they can make lanterns flicker on the surface to point their friends toward clues.
- **Getting them back:** teammates light incense at the shrine to bring a taken player back, once per player per night.
- **Each player chooses for themselves** in the *Stay* ending, so a group can split.

## Map

The village sits on the near shore, not on the water. It's reached by a long causeway over the marsh, and the market boat crosses the lake from the dock once a week. The carp steps are hidden from every house except Sen's, but the cove opens onto the lake, so from the bottom step Sen could see the whole festival on the far shore. Dotted lines show where Bà Thu's lanterns drift each night: to every lost child's house, never to Sen's.

## What to borrow from The Mimic

The Mimic (by MUCDICH, on Roblox since around 2020) is the closest model for Làng Vọng: a story-first horror game built on one country's folklore and released as "books" made of chapters. It's worth studying for structure and restraint, not for its monsters.

| Borrow | How it fits Làng Vọng |
| --- | --- |
| Folklore as identity: it's built on Japanese ghost stories and urban legends | Vietnamese folklore (the ma da, ghost month, Rằm tháng bảy) is just as distinctive, and almost unused on Roblox |
| Books split into chapters, each a contained story that adds to a bigger one | A natural shape for the release plan |
| Restraint over gore: restricted vision, hiding, sound cues, dread | Fits our implied-only approach to what happened in Sen's house |
| Environmental storytelling and memorable character designs that fuel fan theories | Our clue-based puzzle and characters like Bà Thu are made for theory videos |

**Where we differ:** The Mimic leans on chases and survival. Làng Vọng's main challenge is understanding a story, and its ending is emotional rather than an escape. We should keep chases short and rare, so they never drown out the mystery.
