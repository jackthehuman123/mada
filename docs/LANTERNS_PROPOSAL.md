# Lanterns: what could they do? (proposal, 2026-11-02, for Catlyn)

**Status:** proposal, nothing built. Catlyn picks; then it goes into `PROTOTYPE_BRIEF.md` §5.4 and the issues.

## The problem

Today the lanterns are tradition and a hint: on Night 1 (and at 23:00) Bà Thu's four lanterns drift to the four
lost children's houses and light them, and players are meant to notice that the fifth house, Sen's, stays dark.
In play that's a light show you watch, then a "spot the missing thing" hint. The lanterns never do anything, and
the players never do anything with them until the very last scene.

## The idea: one verb, learned all night: fold, name, float

The story already gives the rule: *a lost soul is like a child in the dark; it can't find the way home unless someone
holds up a light* (story.md §2). Every Rằm the village floated lanterns with the lost children's names (§10).
Nobody ever floated one for Sen (§8). The ending is Thu asking the players to *write the name* on her paper fish
(§15).

So make the lantern the players' one tool, used the same way all night: **fold a lantern, write a name on it (or
not), set it on the water.** What it does depends on the name and the place. The true ending becomes the fourth use
of a verb they've used all night, not a mechanic they meet for the first time: they *understand* it. That is the
design's own rule ("understanding, not locks", gameplay.md).

### 1. Read: the lanterns are the clue (replaces "the dark house")

Bà Thu's lanterns carry names. Look at one up close, or film it, and it reads *Minh*, *Hạnh*, *Tí*, *Khoa*. Players
match them to the photos at the shrine and the four houses. Then Bin's tape shows a fifth child, a girl, and **no
lantern has her name**. "Who is she?" (story.md §14) becomes the question the whole group asks, out loud, from
something they read, instead of a house that happens to stay dark.

### 2. Lure: an unnamed lantern pulls Sen away (co-op survival, no combat)

Sen watched the festival from her steps every year and was never allowed to go (§10); her first lantern was the
one she died pushing out (§7). She goes to light on the water. When she's hunting someone (the warning ramp), a
teammate can set an **unnamed** lantern on the water somewhere else and she follows it, and the ramp breaks off.
Paper is scarce (Bà Thu has a little at the shrine; each player carries one or two), so every lure spent is a
choice. It gives players something to *do* when a friend hears the wet footsteps, and it's a reason to stay close.

### 3. Bring home: write a taken friend's name (rescue)

A taken player is lost in the drowned festival under the lanterns. Write their name on a lantern and float it from
the shrine: the light guides them home. This replaces the incense rescue (or incense stays as the way to *light*
the lantern at the shrine). It makes the rule personal: the first name the players ever write is a friend's.

### 4. Guide: the one name nobody wrote (the ending, unchanged)

The letter, Thu's half-finished paper fish, "write to Sen for me", the steps, midnight. Same as now. But by then
players have folded, named and floated lanterns all night, so when Thu holds out the fish they already know what
writing the name will do, and that it has to be *her* name, from *her* steps.

### Optional extras

- **Hold one up for light.** A lit lantern in your hand lights the dark (this answers the flashlight question, and
  the first-person arms from I-12 show it). But it's light near the water: a lone player holding one by the shore
  draws Sen faster. Lantern light is safest together.
- **The ghost lantern on camera.** Through the camcorder only, a lantern drifts out from behind the last house on the
  lane to the middle of the lake: what the puppet master saw and never said (§7). It leads the camera holder to
  the cove.
- **The lake as a clock.** Bà Thu's lanterns gathered in the middle go out one by one through the night; the last at
  midnight. Im lặng is "every lantern on the lake went out at once" (§16), so the clock and the bad ending are the
  same image.

## Writing a name

A small paper panel: type the name. Typing it is the test: you have to *know* it (accept it without diacritics:
"Hanh" = "Hạnh"). Teammates' names can be picked from a list (rescue shouldn't hinge on spelling a username).
