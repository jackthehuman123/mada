## 1. What the story fixes

- A poor village **on a lake**, hard to reach: a **causeway over the marsh** that floods half the rainy season, or the
  weekly **market boat** to the **dock** (§2). The players walk in along the old causeway at dusk (§13).
- An **old shrine by the water**. On Rằm the village carried lanterns from it **all the way round to the puppet stage on
  the far shore** and set them on the lake; they drift to **the middle and gather** "like a little town made of
  light" (§2). Under the middle: the **drowned festival** (§10).
- **Lanes.** Sen's house is **at the very end of the last lane**; the teller lived **three doors down** (§3). Neighbours
  hear each other's radios along the lane (§3); people sit on their front steps in the evening (§3).
- **Behind Sen's house:** a back step (where she folded her paper fish, §6), a **back yard**, a **fence** her voice came
  over (§4), and **old stone steps down into a cove**: reachable only through that yard, **seen from nowhere in the
  village**, and from the bottom step you see **the whole lake, right across to the far shore** (§4). The puppet
  master, on his stage on the far shore, saw a lantern drift out **from behind the last house on the lane** (§7).
- **A well**, where the women met (§5). **Thu's gate** (§12). The **communal house** where the village voted (§11).
- **Inside Sen's house** (§14, §15): no marks on the doorframe; one small bowl and chopsticks, freshly washed; a back
  room with an old woman's chair facing the door and teacups grey with dust; a half-finished paper fish on a shelf;
  the family altar with the ancestors' photos (the letter under it); an old radio hissing (§15).
- **The steps:** little sandals lined up, one pair per lost child; the carp come up (§14). Sen's father's jacket lies in
  the reeds at the bottom of the lake (§11).
- **The shrine now:** the photos the families left, the red paper with every lost child's name but one, Thu's mat
  among the lanterns, tea (§12–§14).
- The village **emptied** after the vote (§11): the houses are abandoned, things left behind.
- **Four lost children's houses:** the teacher's (Minh), the puppet master's (Hạnh), the fish seller's (Tí), the village
  head's (Khoa) (§10, §14).

## 2. The shape

A natural lake, not a circle, with the village wrapped **three quarters of the way round** it and the forest round
everything. Compass for this brief: the far shore is **north**.

      hedge gap  ----- first lane -----  -- middle lane --------   last lane, north

          wild paddies, graves, banyan, silk-cotton tree, roadside shrine
          forest . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . S

- **Lake:** about 420 × 320 studs, irregular: a reedy **marsh arm** in the south-west (the causeway crosses it), the
  shrine's **point** in the west, a wide **south bay** (the dock), a small **inlet** in the east, and a **rocky
  headland** in the north-east that hides the **cove**. The open north is the far shore.
- **The ring path:** one shore path from the shrine, along the south, up the east side, ending at Sen's gate. About
  850 studs: **~70 s** of walking at 12 studs/s end to end (brief: ~60 s).
- **Three lanes = three stretches of that path,** each with its own feel:
  - **First lane (south-west):** the communal house, the village head's house, one empty house. The old centre: wider
    path, brick yards, the communal house's pond.
  - **Middle lane (south):** the dock, the fish seller's, the puppet master's, the teacher's, an empty house. The busiest
    once: fish baskets, a bench at the dock, drying racks.
  - **Last lane (east, running north):** the **well** where it starts, then an empty house, **the teller's old house**,
    two more empty houses, and **Sen's house at the very end**, its back to the headland. Narrower, more overgrown,
    the light thinning out: the lane people stopped walking down.
- **13 buildings:** the communal house, 4 lost children's houses, the teller's, Sen's, 6 empty houses. The empty ones
  sell "the village left": doors open, bundles never taken, a bicycle, sandals by a door.

## 3. Rules the layout keeps (gameplay)

- **The lane is hers to cross, the shore is hers to take.** The path runs **about 35 studs from the water**, just outside
  Sen's 30-stud reach: walking the lane alone is tense, not deadly; **going down to the shore** (the yards, the dock,
  the steps) alone is where she takes you.
- **Houses face the lake**, front yards running down toward the water. Each lost child's house has its **lantern stop on
  the shore below it** (§12). Sen's has none.
- **Sightlines:** every house can see across the water to houses on the other side; from the shrine you see the whole
  ring, so on Night 1 the **one dark house** at the far end is a gap in a line of lit windows. The **cove** sits
  behind the headland's rock spur: invisible from every house, the lane and the shrine (§4); its mouth opens toward
  the middle and the far shore (§4, §7).
- **The safe place is the shrine** on its point (`ShrineZone`), warm and full of lanterns.
- **No way out:** the causeway floods after Night 1; the hedge gaps lead into the fields and the forest, where
  "Wandering in the forest won't save you." brings you back. The flooded path round to the far shore is cut.
- Every tag and Location name stays (`Shrine`, `TeacherHouse`, … `CarpSteps`, `Dock`, `Causeway`, `DrownedFestival`);
  positions move.

## 4. The places, walking round

**The causeway and the hedge gap.** A raised dirt causeway over black marsh water, reeds, dead melaleuca trunks. It
reaches land at a gap in the bamboo hedge: a weathered gate post, a water jar, the first red lantern. This is where
players spawn and the first thing they see is the lake opening up in front of them.

**The shrine.** On a low stone point out into the lake, open-sided, four faded red pillars, a low curved roof. The
altar faces the water; rows of the families' photos; the red paper (§11); Thu's straw mat, a teapot, cups; lanterns
everywhere, finished and half-folded. Steps down to the water where the lanterns are set out. Bin's camera floats in
the reeds just off the point (§13).

**The communal house (đình).** The biggest building: a raised brick platform, heavy columns, a big tiled roof with
curled corners, open front, a half-lotus pond and a brick yard in front. Benches inside, set out for the vote (§11).

**The lost children's houses** (one storey, three bays, tiled roof, porch on posts, brick front yard, a small kitchen hut
beside, a water jar, banana and areca trees, a low wall or bamboo fence, a gate):
- **The village head's:** the best kept: a whitewashed gate, a writing desk, papers (the minutes, the shrine list).
- **The fish seller's:** by the dock; baskets, nets, scales, a stall table at the gate.
- **The puppet master's:** water puppets hanging from the rafters, a paint table, a small practice tank (§14 diary).
- **The teacher's:** a slate, a desk with a drawer, books, a class photo on the wall (§14).

**The dock.** A wooden jetty into the south bay, a few planks missing, a rotting boat half sunk beside it; the market
boat no longer comes.

**The well.** Where the last lane begins: a round brick well, a rope and two buckets. Where Thu and the teller met (§5).

**The teller's old house.** Three doors from Sen's (§3). Empty: a radio on the table, the dial turned up (§3).

**Sen's house.** At the very end of the last lane, the dead end: the lane stops at her gate. Greyer than the others, the
plaster flaking, shutters closed. Inside (§14, §15): the doorframe without marks, one bowl and chopsticks, the
family altar and ancestors' photos (the letter beneath), the old radio hissing, the back room with Thu's chair facing
the door and the dusty cups, the half-finished paper fish on the shelf. The **back step** (§6), the **back yard** with
its fence, then through bamboo and rocks to the **seven stone steps** (decided at M5) down into the cove: the sandals
lined up (§14), the carp, the water.

**The cove and the bottom step.** A small hidden pocket of water inside the headland's rocks. From the bottom step the
rocks open on one side and **the whole lake lies in front of you**: the lanterns gathered in the middle, the far shore,
the puppet stage (§4). The game's most important view.

**The far shore.** Across the open north: a flat festival ground, tattered flag poles, lantern posts, and the **puppet
stage on stilts** over the water (§2, §7). Only ever seen: a dark silhouette by day-for-night, a few cold lights.

**The middle of the lake.** Where the lanterns gather; Sen drifts here (I-16); the drowned festival is underneath.

**Behind the village.** The bamboo hedge, then the wild paddies with graves, the banyan with the red cloth, the bare
silk-cotton tree, the roadside shrine (miếu), the ox cart, and the forest (`FOREST_PROPOSAL.md`).

## 5. The views to build for

Design each of these as a shot: stand there in Studio with the camera and make it look right.

1. **Arrival** (Night 1, dusk): from the causeway's end through the hedge gap: the lake opens, the shrine on its point,
   lantern light, the ring of roofs across the water. "This is the place."
2. **From the shrine:** the whole ring of the village across the water, the far shore dark beyond. On Night 1, the
   lanterns drifting out from here to the houses, and windows lighting one by one, with **the one gap** at the end.
3. **Down the lane:** houses on one side, the lake through the yards on the other, the hedge behind, red lanterns
   at the gates; empty houses with their doors open.
4. **The last lane:** a narrowing corridor of dark, empty houses with **Sen's house closing the end of it**; the well
   at its start.
5. **The bottom step:** the reveal of the whole lake, the lanterns, the far shore (§4). Also the finale's view.
6. **The village edge** (Catlyn's reference): from a hedge gap, the wet path out, the paddies and graves, the banyan,
   the forest, the full moon. Also the proposed **menu background**.
7. **Across the water at Sen's house** from the middle lane: the dark house at the end, the headland behind it, the
   cove's mouth a black notch in the rocks.
8. **The far shore** from anywhere on the ring: the puppet stage's silhouette on its stilts.

## 6. Light and sound by place

| Place | Light | Sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shrine | the warmest place: many lanterns, candle glow | lanterns rustling, water lapping, Thu's tea |
| Lane, first/middle | red lanterns at gates, lit windows once their lantern arrives | crickets, frogs, a door creaking |
| Last lane | fewer lights, darker, mist | quieter; the radio hiss near Sen's house |
| Sen's house | dark; moonlight through shutters | the old radio's static |
| Cove / steps | moonlight on water, the lanterns far off | carp at the surface, dripping, very quiet |
| Lake middle | the lanterns' town of light | very faint festival drums under the water |
| Hedge / forest | cold blue-grey, low fog, no warm light | bamboo creaking, then nothing |

Whole map: a big full moon (Rằm), cold desaturated blue-grey; warm reds only where people were.
